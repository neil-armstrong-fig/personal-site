## Matsuyama, Imabari, and the Shimanami Kaido

On the rest day I went to Matsuyama, somewhere I don't think a lot of tourists actually visit. You can see the building said to be the inspiration for Spirited Away's bathhouse there. And of course, another amazing castle, plus a pretty cool clock.

![A traditional multi-storey wooden bathhouse building with dark timber and white walls, said to have inspired the bathhouse in the film Spirited Away](./_assets/dogo-onsen-bathhouse-building.jpg "The inspiration for Spirited Away's bathhouse.")

![Matsuyama Castle's white stone walls and turret rising above trees, with visitors walking near the base](./_assets/matsuyama-castle-entrance-walls.jpg "Another amazing castle.")

![A view down a tree-lined approach path from Matsuyama Castle over the city below](./_assets/view-down-from-matsuyama-castle.jpg "Another amazing castle.")

![An ornate wooden karakuri clock tower on a Matsuyama street corner, with a red umbrella and modern buildings behind](./_assets/matsuyama-karakuri-clock-tower.jpg "A pretty cool clock.")

Though I did kind of like the vibe of the castle in the place I was staying in Imabari, too. Just the one nighttime look at what I was going to tackle tomorrow...

![Imabari Castle's white walls and turrets illuminated at night, reflected in the surrounding moat](./_assets/imabari-castle-illuminated-at-night.jpg "What I was going to tackle tomorrow.")

![A dark night view over hills and water, with the faint silhouette of the islands to be crossed the next day](./_assets/night-view-of-islands-to-cross.jpg "What I was going to tackle tomorrow.")

Today was guaranteed to be a treat: the famous Shimanami Kaido, an incredible cycle route through a bunch of islands in the Seto Inland Sea. I expected this to be a highlight of the trip, and it certainly didn't disappoint. One of the best things about it: there's a transport service for bikepacking bags, so I could take a good bit of weight off the bike to really enjoy it.

![The Imabari Cycle Station building with loaded touring bikes parked outside and a cyclist walking past](./_assets/imabari-cycle-station-with-bikes.jpg "Ready for the Shimanami Kaido.")

The first challenge was a windy bridge, the Kurushima-Kaikyo Bridge, which offered some incredible views already. Right after it I'd added a bonus climb for a nice view and the lay of the land - what I was going to be tackling today, and a great view of the bridge I'd just crossed.

![Cycling across a long suspension bridge on the Shimanami Kaido, with islands visible ahead](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/cycling-across-shimanami-suspension-bridge.mp4 "The first challenge was a windy bridge.")

![A tall white suspension tower of the Kurushima-Kaikyo Bridge against a cloudy sky, with the cycle path curving below and a car crossing the road deck](./_assets/kurushima-kaikyo-bridge-tower.jpg "The first challenge was a windy bridge.")

![A curving road at a hilltop viewpoint overlooking scattered islands in the Seto Inland Sea](./_assets/hilltop-viewpoint-over-seto-islands.jpg "The lay of the land.")

![A wider hilltop view over the Seto Inland Sea's islands under a bright sky](./_assets/wide-hilltop-view-seto-islands.jpg "The lay of the land.")

The cycling infrastructure here, especially compared to the rest of the roads I'd been on so far, really did make it feel like a cycling theme park: great cycle lanes everywhere, and these incredible bridges weaving their way across the islands.

![Cycling along a dedicated cycle lane beside a large road bridge, with other cyclists visible ahead](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/cycle-lane-beside-shimanami-road-bridge.mp4 "A cycling theme park.")

![A cycle path curving around a coastal headland with the sea alongside](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/coastal-road-curving-round-headland.mp4 "These incredible bridges weaving their way across the islands.")

![A small harbour with moored boats beneath a conical island hill on the Shimanami Kaido route](./_assets/harbour-with-boats-and-conical-island.jpg "These incredible bridges weaving their way across the islands.")

![Cycling along a seafront path beside calm blue water and distant hills](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/cycling-seafront-path-calm-water.mp4 "Amazing blue waters.")

Now, you can probably notice in the videos if you had the sound on, it was really windy (what a surprise 😂). It was coming from the west, and my route snaked north, west, north, east, north, west, so I got the joy of headwind, a super fast tailwind, and then a headwind to finish. Thankfully I didn't have too many moments of dodgy bike handling from the crosswinds, but it did add an extra edge to the cycling 😂. Generally it's a bit of a cheat code to get a great view from a bridge in Japan, but the Shimanami Kaido takes that to the next level.

![Riding along the cycle lane of a long cable-stayed bridge, its white pylons and cables framing the islands ahead](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/riding-cable-stayed-bridge-cycle-lane.mp4 "A cheat code to get a great view from a bridge.")

Though very often it's the bridge itself that is the view, they're just stunning.

![A cable-stayed bridge over glittering water, seen with the sun low over the sea](./_assets/cable-stayed-bridge-sun-over-water.jpg "The bridge itself is the view.")

![Small green islands scattered across calm blue water, seen from a Shimanami Kaido bridge.](./_assets/islands-scattered-across-blue-sea.jpg "Just stunning.")

And of course, of course, at the end of this incredible cycling theme park of a route, it ends with a ferry.

![Looking out from the ferry deck at a shoreline town in the evening light](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/ferry-deck-view-of-shoreline-town.mp4 "It ends with a ferry.")

I want to come back to do this again (and maybe again, again!). It was incredible. The only issue: I nearly think the mountain stage the day before was better. But perhaps that was just the cloudy weather.

## Hiroshima and Itsukushima

Onwards to Hiroshima. This was one of those days where, thanks to strict planning, I had to commit to cycling through in the rain. Once you get a certain amount of wet from the rain, it starts to bother you less.

![A calm reservoir surrounded by green hills, with a small red marker buoy floating on the water](./_assets/calm-reservoir-with-red-buoy.jpg "Nice views before the rain properly started.")

![Cherry blossom trees along a paved riverside path, with houses in the background under an overcast sky](./_assets/cherry-blossoms-along-riverside-path.jpg "Nice views before the rain properly started.")

![A wooden shrine gate and small shrine building beside a parked car in a residential street](./_assets/small-roadside-shrine-with-torii-gate.jpg "Nice views before the rain properly started.")

![Riding a quiet curving road through green hills before the rain set in](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/quiet-road-through-hills-before-rain.mp4 "Nice views before the rain properly started.")

But yes, it did start to rain pretty heavily.

![Rows of parked bicycles beside a wet road under a grey, rainy sky](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/parked-bicycles-in-the-rain.mp4 "Rain pretty heavily.")

Still quite beautiful in the hills, though I imagine on a clear day it would have been magnificent.

![A wet road running beside a railway line and houses, with misty hills beyond](./_assets/wet-road-beside-railway-in-mist.jpg "Still quite beautiful in the hills.")

![Parked bicycles beneath a lamppost with foggy hills and buildings in the background](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/bicycles-in-foggy-hillside-town.mp4 "Still quite beautiful in the hills.")

I survived. Now, a few days' rest in Hiroshima.

The first rest day I took it pretty easy and went to the Peace Memorial Museum, which is fantastic, though obviously not a fantastic subject. Otherwise, just a generally relaxing day.

![Small architectural models of Hiroshima buildings displayed in the Peace Memorial Museum, with an information plaque in front](./_assets/museum-architectural-models-display.jpg "The Peace Memorial Museum.")

![The Atomic Bomb Dome's skeletal ruins surrounded by modern Hiroshima buildings on the riverbank](./_assets/atomic-bomb-dome-daytime-riverbank.jpg "The Peace Memorial Museum.")

![The Hiroshima Peace Memorial (Atomic Bomb Dome) illuminated at dusk, reflected in the river](./_assets/atomic-bomb-dome-at-dusk.jpg "The Atomic Bomb Dome at dusk.")

Day two had, you guessed it, a ferry.

![A calm sea view with a hazy coastline and small islands on the horizon](./_assets/calm-sea-view-hazy-coastline-islands.jpg "A ferry, day two.")

It was kind of a speedboat thing with seats, tbh, I should have taken a picture. It brought me to the island of Itsukushima. I can't recommend it enough. I started with a hike, as it was pretty busy near the town, so it was nice to get back to some peace.

![Mossy stone steps scattered with fallen cherry blossom petals on the hike up Itsukushima](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/mossy-steps-with-cherry-petals.mp4 "Back to some peace.")

![A hazy view over a bay and hills from the hiking trail on Itsukushima](./_assets/hazy-view-over-bay-from-hillside.jpg "Back to some peace.")

![An ornate shrine building with two guardian statues flanking the entrance](./_assets/shrine-entrance-guardian-statues.jpg "Back to some peace.")

Coming down, I got some snacks, a beer or two, and enjoyed the view of the Otorii gate, sunset just amplifying its aesthetic.

![The Itsukushima floating torii gate seen across exposed mudflats at low tide, with people walking out to it](./_assets/otorii-gate-across-low-tide-mudflats.jpg "The Otorii gate at low tide.")

![The large orange Otorii gate standing on the exposed mudflats, with visitors gathered at its base](./_assets/otorii-gate-close-view-with-visitors.jpg "The Otorii gate at low tide.")

![A hand holding a drink can in silhouette against the sun setting over the bay](./_assets/hand-holding-drink-at-sunset-beach.jpg "Snacks, a beer or two.")

![The Otorii gate silhouetted against a vivid orange sunset, with visitors gathered on the sand nearby](./_assets/otorii-gate-silhouette-at-sunset.jpg "Sunset amplifying its aesthetic.")

Back on the road! Of course starting with a nice view from a bridge.

![A view along a river through a small town, with houses on the bank and a road bridge crossing](./_assets/river-view-through-small-town.jpg "A nice view from a bridge.")

Though I also stumbled upon a bridge that is better looked at than looked from.

![A touring bike leaning against the railing overlooking an arched wooden bridge crossing a wide river.](./_assets/touring-bike-overlooking-arched-wooden-bridge.jpg "A bridge better looked at than looked from.")

![The multi-arched wooden Kintai Bridge crossing a wide river, with hills in the background](./_assets/multi-arched-wooden-bridge-over-river.jpg "A bridge better looked at than looked from.")

Something that cycling in Japan provides, unbeatable on all my trips, is just the pure peace of their towns; I've talked about the quiet, but it always hits me, and there are constant incredible views, generally from the top of a bridge.

![Cycling along a quiet road beside flooded rice paddies and houses in a peaceful town](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/quiet-road-beside-rice-paddies.mp4 "The pure peace of their towns.")

![An ornate orange lamp post on a red bridge railing, overlooking a river and hills](./_assets/ornate-lamp-post-on-red-bridge.jpg "Constant incredible views, generally from the top of a bridge.")

![A river valley stretching toward hazy hills under a bright midday sun](./_assets/river-valley-toward-hazy-hills.jpg "Constant incredible views.")

I'd just gawk at the hills constantly (partly what inspired a future trip), though the camera rarely does it justice.

![A quiet paved road running between rice paddies towards distant hills, filmed while riding](/trips/tokyo-to-seoul/farm-road-through-rice-paddies-towards-hills.mp4 "Gawking at the hills constantly.")
